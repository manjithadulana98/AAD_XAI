How to use this file
- Each run/experiment gets a new dated entry appended at the end.
- Record: method/protocol, key settings, and mean accuracy (± std) + what you’re comparing against.
- If you compare across protocols (different CV, lags, overlap), note “not directly comparable”.

Experiment: StimulusGAT v4 (time-resolved FiLM) — DTU subject-independent LOSO, paired TRF
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_loso_sgat_v4_time_resolved.py (Kaggle kernel

Method / Protocol
- CV: leave_one_subject_out(all_trials, seed=42), 18 folds; 1 random val subject per fold
- Model: StimulusGAT with TimeResolvedFiLM (commit d00c768), best-val-accuracy checkpoint
- Key preprocessing: PreprocessConfig defaults (64 Hz), 5 s windows, no overlap,
  per-window per-channel EEG standardization (SGAT only)
- Key hyperparams: AdamW lr=1e-3, wd=1e-4, batch 64, 25 epochs, 1 seed per fold
- Baseline: backward TRF on the SAME folds (tmin 0, tmax 0.4 s, alpha=100 fixed,
  broadband envelope), via WindowedEEGAudioDataset

Results (mean ± std across 18 test subjects)
- StimulusGAT v4:                          0.6286 ± 0.0487
- StimulusGAT v4, envelopes shifted 2.5 s: 0.4988 ± 0.0195  (alignment control)
- TRF, same folds:                         0.5997 ± 0.0408
- SGAT − TRF: +0.0289, SGAT better on 16/18 subjects, Wilcoxon p=0.0020
- SGAT − shifted: +0.1297, Wilcoxon p=7.6e-06
- Previous StimulusGAT LOSO: v1 0.4887, v2 0.4814, v3 0.4952 (all chance)

Notes
- Alignment control: shifting both envelopes by 2.5 s keeps every time-averaged
  speech statistic but breaks EEG alignment; accuracy falls to chance on every
  subject (0.475–0.538). The model decodes by tracking, not by a shortcut
  (candidate A is always the male speaker).
- The TRF baseline uses a fixed alpha; a per-fold tuned TRF is needed before
  claiming StimulusGAT beats linear decoding in general.
- The 0.6328 TRF figure above is WITHIN-subject (story/speaker-out, subband
  envelope, tuned alpha) and is not comparable to subject-independent LOSO.
- Architecture and lr were developed on a 14-subject split whose test subjects
  (S10, S17) are part of this LOSO: mild adaptivity, single config, one seed.

Entry date: 2026-10-27
Experiment: StimulusGAT v4 robustness — 3 seeds vs. validation-tuned TRF, same LOSO folds
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_loso_sgat_v4_seeds_tuned_trf.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/stimulusgat-loso-v4-seeds-tuned-trf, code at commit f069059)
- loso_v4_seeds_tuned_trf.json (Kaggle output; seeds 43/44 + TRF grid per fold)
  combined offline with results/loso_v4_results.json (seed 42); both untracked

Method / Protocol
- CV: identical folds to the 2026-10-26 entry (leave_one_subject_out, seed=42;
  val subjects verified equal fold by fold)
- StimulusGAT: unchanged v4 config; new seeds 43, 44 (+ seed 42 from v4)
- TRF: backward ridge, lags 0-0.4 s, 19 alphas (1 .. 1e9, half-decade steps)
  x {raw, per-window standardized EEG}; selected per fold by max attended-
  envelope correlation on the fold's validation subject. Closed-form ridge path
  from accumulated normal equations (matches sklearn Ridge to <4e-6 rel.)

Results (mean ± std across 18 test subjects)
- StimulusGAT seed 42 / 43 / 44:    0.6286 / 0.6258 / 0.6272
- StimulusGAT 3-seed mean:          0.6272 ± 0.0511
- Shifted-envelope control, 3 seeds: 0.4988 / 0.4956 / 0.5040 (all folds 0.467–0.552)
- TRF, val-tuned:                   0.5988 ± 0.0443
- TRF, alpha=100 raw (this solver): 0.6027   (v4 TRFDecoder: 0.5997)
- TRF, best single config chosen on TEST (oracle, not legitimate): 0.6036
- TRF, best config per test subject (oracle, upward-biased):        0.6172
- SGAT mean − TRF tuned:   +0.0284, better on 13/18, Wilcoxon p=0.0057
- SGAT mean − TRF a100:    +0.0245, better on 12/18, p=0.0079
- SGAT mean − per-subject oracle TRF: +0.0100, 10/18, p=0.33
- Each seed alone vs TRF tuned: p = 0.008 / 0.020 / 0.003
- Seed variability: within-subject SD across seeds 0.014 (max 0.045), below
  the 600-window binomial SE (~0.020); seed accuracies correlate 0.85–0.96
  across subjects. Subject effects dominate; seeds do not.

Notes
- The v4 gain survives a tuned baseline and two more seeds: ~+2.8 points over
  linear backward decoding, with the alignment control at chance for all seeds.
- Tuning did not help the TRF (0.599 tuned vs 0.603 fixed); validation
  correlation picked heavy regularisation (alpha 1e4–3e7) that did not raise
  accuracy. Even the best of 38 TRF configs picked on the test set reaches
  only 0.604, so this TRF family is at its ceiling; the baseline was not
  undertuned. Caveat: richer linear baselines (subband envelopes, wider lags,
  CCA) were not tried.
- alpha=100 here does not reproduce v4's TRFDecoder per subject (16/18 differ,
  max 0.018) though means agree to 0.003. Most likely cause: lags are built
  within each 5 s window here vs. across concatenated windows in TRFDecoder.
- One validation subject per fold makes model selection noisy: SGAT best
  epochs range 1–25, and fold S1 (val S11, a near-chance subject) stopped at
  epoch 1 for seeds 43/44 (test 0.550/0.612 vs 0.637 for seed 42).
- Same adaptivity caveat as v4 (architecture developed on a split containing
  S10/S17). Not comparable to the within-subject 0.6328 above.

Entry date: 2026-10-27
Experiment: Story-disjoint round 1 — SGAT base vs. 50%-overlap training windows vs. tuned TRF
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v5.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v5-round1, code at commit 949f485)
- story_disjoint_v5.json (Kaggle output; per-fold, per-subject counts; untracked)

Method / Protocol
- CV: subject_group_story_out (seed 42): 6 subject groups of 3 x 3 story folds
  (content groups 1 / 2 / 3+4). Train = other subjects on the other stories;
  2 val subjects scored on the held-out stories; test subjects scored on the
  held-out stories only. Every two-talker trial tested exactly once (600
  windows per subject); integrity asserted for all 18 folds.
- StimulusGAT: v4 config unchanged, seed 42. Arms: base (non-overlapping 5 s
  training windows) and overlap (2.5 s hop: 4870 -> 9253 windows in fold 1).
  Epoch chosen by pooled val accuracy.
- TRF: same ridge grid as the robustness entry, chosen by val correlation;
  extra arms chosen by val accuracy and raw EEG with lags 0-0.25 vs 0-0.4 s.
- Pre-registered rule: keep overlap iff mean pooled val accuracy beats base.

Results (mean ± std across 18 test subjects; each pooled over its 3 folds)
- TRF tuned (val corr):    0.5984 ± 0.0399
- TRF tuned (val acc):     0.5940;  raw lags 0-0.4 s 0.5963, 0-0.25 s 0.5938
- SGAT base:               0.6265 ± 0.0498   shifted control 0.5099
- SGAT overlap:            0.6305 ± 0.0429   shifted control 0.5097
- SGAT base − TRF:     +0.0281, better on 12/18, Wilcoxon p=0.024
- SGAT overlap − TRF:  +0.0321, better on 16/18, p=0.0012
- Overlap − base:      +0.0040, better on 8/18, p=0.78
- TRF lags 0.4 − 0.25: +0.0025, 10/18, p=0.49
- Val accuracy (mean over folds): base 0.6431, overlap 0.6476 (overlap higher in
  11 folds, lower in 6, tied in 1) -> overlap KEPT by the pre-registered rule
- Per held-out story fold (pooled): TRF / base / overlap
  G1 0.605/0.624/0.628, G2 0.598/0.639/0.633, G3+4 0.591/0.620/0.631
- All 18 subjects above chance with overlap (per-subject binomial p<0.05,
  windows treated as independent)

Comparison with plain LOSO (same 18 subjects, paired)
- SGAT story-disjoint base − plain LOSO (seeds 43/44 mean): +0.0000, p=0.95
- TRF story-disjoint − plain LOSO tuned TRF: −0.0004, p=0.86
- SGAT − TRF gap: 0.0277 (LOSO) vs 0.0281 (story-disjoint)

Notes
- Hearing other subjects' EEG for the same stories did not inflate plain LOSO:
  both models score the same when train and test share no stories, with ~45%
  less training data per fold (13 subjects x ~2/3 of the stories vs 16
  subjects x all stories). The
  SGAT advantage over a tuned linear decoder survives the stricter protocol.
- Doubling the training windows by overlap did not measurably help (test +0.4
  points, n.s.). It is kept per the rule, but the val gain is also small and
  noisy. Read with the point above: more windows of the same kind are not the
  limiting factor at this accuracy.
- Lags beyond 250 ms add nothing measurable to the TRF (consistent with the
  synthetic delay sweep).
- Shifted control is slightly above 0.5 (0.510; 12/18 subjects, Wilcoxon
  p=0.11; pooled binomial p=0.04 but windows within a trial are not
  independent). Plain LOSO gave 0.496–0.504. Not significant at subject level;
  a possible small talker-identity cue in the shifted envelopes. Watch it in
  later rounds.
- Single seed; within-subject seed SD was 0.014 under LOSO, so the 0.004
  overlap−base difference is well inside seed noise.

Entry date: 2026-10-27
Experiment: Story-disjoint round 2 — EEG band 1-8 Hz -> 1-31 Hz (envelope kept at 1-8 Hz)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v6.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v6-round2, code at commit fbcffbf)
- story_disjoint_v6.json (Kaggle output; untracked). Compared offline with
  round 1's story_disjoint_v5.json (fold ids, test/val subjects and held-out
  stories verified identical for all 18 folds)

Method / Protocol
- Single factor vs round-1 "overlap" arm: PreprocessConfig(bandpass_hz=(1, 31),
  envelope_bandpass_hz=(1, 8)), 64 Hz. Same folds, StimulusGAT config, seed 42,
  2.5 s training hop. TRF baseline (1-8 Hz) reused from round 1.
- Motivation: the frequency encoder's 8 bands span 1-32 Hz, but with 1-8 Hz
  EEG the six bands above 8 Hz saw only filter roll-off.
- Pre-registered rule: keep 1-31 Hz iff mean pooled val accuracy beats 0.6476
  and the shift control is not above chance (Wilcoxon vs 0.5, p >= 0.05).

Results (mean ± std across 18 test subjects)
- SGAT wide (1-31 Hz):  0.6276 ± 0.0523   shifted 0.5077 (10/18 above 0.5, p=0.36)
- SGAT round-1 overlap: 0.6305            shifted 0.5097
- TRF (1-8 Hz):         0.5984
- Wide − overlap: −0.0029, better on 7/18, Wilcoxon p=0.68
- Wide − TRF:     +0.0292, better on 12/18, p=0.033
- Val: 0.6500 vs 0.6476 (higher in 10 folds, lower in 8) -> KEEP by the rule

Notes
- Null result: alpha/beta EEG adds no measurable accuracy. The decodable
  information this model uses sits in the 1-8 Hz tracking band.
- The rule says keep, but the val margin (+0.24 points, 10 vs 8 folds) is noise.
  The practical reason to keep 1-31 Hz is interpretability: the frequency-band
  contributions CLAUDE.md asks for are only meaningful if the bands carry
  signal. The shift control did not rise, so no EEG-only shortcut appeared.
- Method lesson: a "beats" rule with no margin turns ties into decisions.
  From round 3 on, a change is kept only if val accuracy improves by at least
  0.5 points; otherwise the simpler/previous setting stays.

Entry date: 2026-10-27
Experiment: Story-disjoint round 3 — power-law sub-band speech envelope (TRF and StimulusGAT)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v7.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v7-round3, code at commit 074ffe9)
- story_disjoint_v7.json (Kaggle output; untracked). Compared offline with
  rounds 1-2 (fold ids and meta verified identical for all 18 folds)

Method / Protocol
- Single factor: envelope_method="powerlaw_subbands" (15-band gammatone at
  8 kHz, |x|^0.6, summed, 1-8 Hz, z-scored per trial) instead of the Hilbert
  envelope. Still one envelope per talker; architecture unchanged.
- TRF: 1-8 Hz EEG, same ridge grid/selection as round 1; compared with round
  1's Hilbert TRF. StimulusGAT: 1-31 Hz EEG, 2.5 s hop, seed 42; compared with
  round 2's wide_overlap.
- Sanity check: power-law vs Hilbert envelope r = 0.829 on aske_story1_trial_1.
- Pre-registered: SGAT keeps it iff val gain >= 0.005 and shift control at
  chance; the TRF baseline uses whichever envelope has higher TRF val accuracy.

Results (mean ± std across 18 test subjects)
- TRF power-law:   0.6464 ± 0.0739  (Hilbert 0.5984)  +0.0480, 14/18, p=0.0008
- SGAT power-law:  0.6503 ± 0.0610  (Hilbert 0.6276)  +0.0227, 14/18, p=0.0018
- SGAT − TRF, both power-law:  +0.0039, better on 10/18, p=0.50
- (SGAT − TRF, both Hilbert, round 2: +0.0292, 12/18, p=0.033)
- Gain difference TRF − SGAT: +0.025, 11/18, p=0.074; per-subject gains
  correlate only 0.24
- Shift control: 0.5002 (9/18 above 0.5, p=0.78)
- Val: TRF 0.5981 -> 0.6458 (higher in 16/18 folds); SGAT 0.6500 -> 0.6719
  (+0.022, higher in 15/18) -> power-law KEPT for both models
- TRF lag 0-400 vs 0-250 with power-law: +0.0037, p=0.40

Notes
- Best absolute accuracy so far: SGAT 0.650 on story-disjoint DTU, 5 s windows.
- But the StimulusGAT advantage over the linear decoder disappears once both
  get the better envelope (+2.9 -> +0.4 points, n.s.). Most likely, part of
  StimulusGAT's earlier edge came from its speech encoder partly compensating
  for a poor Hilbert envelope. A good input representation removes that edge.
- Do NOT report SGAT power-law vs TRF Hilbert (+5.2 points) as the model's
  gain: that mixes the envelope change with the model change.
- TRF baseline for later rounds: power-law envelope, 1-8 Hz EEG.

Entry date: 2026-10-28
Experiment: Story-disjoint round 4 — multi-band speech input (15 sub-band envelopes, TRF and StimulusGAT)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v8.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v8-round4, code at commit 92d7aca)
- story_disjoint_v8.json (Kaggle output; untracked). Compared offline with
  round 3 (fold ids and meta verified identical for all 18 folds)

Method / Protocol
- Single factor: envelope_method="powerlaw_subbands_multi". Same 15
  power-law gammatone bands as round 3, but not summed: each band is
  z-scored, giving env (2, 15, T).
- StimulusGAT(speech_bands=15): the shared speech encoder's first conv reads
  all 15 bands (architecture change approved by the user).
- TRF: multi-output backward ridge (one shared alpha); decision = mean over
  bands of corr(reconstruction_k, candidate band k).
- Everything else as in round 3.
- Sanity checks passed:
  - the multi-output TRF at K=1 reproduces the single-band TRF;
  - the z-scored sum of the raw bands matches the round-3 envelope (r > 0.999).
- Pre-registered:
  - SGAT keeps multi-band iff val >= 0.6719 + 0.005 AND the shift control is
    at chance;
  - the TRF baseline is whichever of {summed, multi} has the higher TRF val acc.

Results (mean across 18 test subjects, each pooled over its 3 folds)
- TRF multi-band 0.6573 vs summed 0.6464: +0.0109, 13/18, p=0.0065.
  - Val 0.6517 vs 0.6458 (higher in 13/18 folds).
  - -> multi-band TRF is the NEW BASELINE.
- SGAT multi-band 0.6779 vs summed 0.6503: +0.0276, 12/18, p=0.029.
  - Val 0.6831 vs 0.6719 (+0.011).
- SGAT multi vs TRF multi: +0.0205, 12/18, p=0.19.
- SHIFT CONTROL (envelopes rolled by 2.5 s): 0.5449 vs 0.5002 in round 3
  (rise p=0.047; vs chance p=0.139).
  - Folds with test-shift >= 0.6: sgso_g3_k0 0.825, sgso_g3_k1 0.837,
    sgso_g4_k1 0.657. In these folds shifted accuracy ~= real accuracy, and
    val was 0.84-0.87 at epoch 1.
  - Subjects affected (shifted acc): S3 0.68, S6 0.74, S11 0.61, S14 0.74.
- Excluding those 3 folds (post hoc):
  - SGAT multi - SGAT sum: -0.005, p=0.92;
  - SGAT multi - TRF multi: -0.013, p=0.46;
  - val: multi 0.6652 vs sum 0.6667.

Decision
- The letter of the rule passes: val +1.1 points, shift vs chance p=0.14.
- But the shift control shows the SGAT gain is a SHORTCUT, not better envelope
  tracking:
  - in 3 folds the model predicts as well with misaligned speech as with
    aligned speech;
  - with those folds removed, the gain is zero.
  - The subject-level Wilcoxon was underpowered for a shortcut confined to a
    few subjects and stories. Validation itself was contaminated, because the
    val subjects of those folds benefited too.
- -> multi-band input REJECTED for StimulusGAT in its current form. SGAT
  stays on the summed power-law envelope (round 3).
- Kept: the multi-band TRF as the baseline.
- Standing: SGAT (r3) 0.6503 vs TRF multi 0.6573: -0.0071, 9/18, p=0.73
  (tie; the TRF is nominally ahead).

Notes
- Likely mechanism (hypothesis, untested): the 15-band input lets the speech
  encoder tell the male talker from the female one regardless of timing.
  Combined with any EEG cue that correlates with the attended talker in some
  stories (e.g. 1-31 Hz alpha lateralisation via the attended side), the model
  can score talker identity instead of tracking. The summed envelope (r3) gave
  shift 0.500, so that input did not allow it.
- Lesson for the protocol: also check per-fold shift accuracy (e.g. any fold
  with shift >= 0.6 is a flag), not only the subject-level test vs 0.5.
- Fixes would need sign-off, because each is a data or architecture change:
  - talker-invariant band input (per-window band normalisation, or removing
    the band profile);
  - a training-time shift-negative (an extra loss, which needs approval).

Entry date: 2026-10-29
Experiment: Story-disjoint round 5 — per-subject Euclidean alignment (EA) of the EEG (TRF and StimulusGAT)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v9.py (Kaggle kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v9-round5, code at commit 36fe00f)
- story_disjoint_v9.json (Kaggle output; untracked). Compared offline with
  rounds 3 and 4 (fold ids and meta verified identical for all 18 folds)

Method / Protocol
- Single factor: src/aad_xai/data/alignment.py. Each subject's EEG trials are
  mapped to R^{-1/2} X, where R = that subject's mean trial covariance plus a
  ridge of 1e-3 x mean eigenvalue (average-referenced EEG has rank 63/64).
- References are computed per fold and per split:
  - training subjects use their training-story trials;
  - val/test subjects use their held-out-story trials.
  - Unlabelled EEG only, but TRANSDUCTIVE for test subjects: state this
    whenever the result is reported.
- On-Kaggle check (S1, 60 trials): aligned eigenvalues median 0.974, max 1.000;
  2 of 63 damped below 0.9 by the ridge; common mode stays ~0 (5e-13).
- TRF: round-4 config (1-8 Hz EEG, 15-band envelope, multi-output ridge).
- SGAT: round-3 config (1-31 Hz EEG, summed power-law envelope, 2.5 s hop,
  seed 42).
- Pre-registered:
  - SGAT keeps EA iff val >= 0.6719 + 0.005 AND the shift control is at
    chance (subject p >= 0.05 AND no fold with shift >= 0.6);
  - the TRF baseline is whichever of {no EA, EA} has the higher val.

Results (mean across 18 test subjects, each pooled over its 3 folds)
- SGAT + EA 0.6773 vs round 3 0.6503: +0.0270, 13/18, p=0.0096.
  - Val 0.6904 vs 0.6719 (higher in 12/18 folds).
- TRF + EA 0.6673 vs round 4 0.6573: +0.0099, 9/18, p=0.16.
  - Val 0.6739 vs 0.6517 (higher in 12/18).
- Shift control 0.5040 (vs 0.5 p=0.55); no fold >= 0.6 (max 0.529).
- -> EA KEPT for SGAT; the TRF baseline = TRF + EA.
- MAIN: SGAT + EA vs TRF + EA: +0.0100, 12/18, p=0.37 (not significant).
- Exploratory (not pre-registered): SGAT gains more from EA than the TRF does
  (+0.017, 14/18, p=0.017).
- Across-subject SD: SGAT 0.061 -> 0.045. The weakest subjects gain most:
  S11 +0.110, S12 +0.094, S16 +0.068, S17 +0.062.

Notes
- Best accuracy so far: SGAT 0.677 on story-disjoint DTU (5 s windows).
  - The SGAT gain is significant and the shift control is clean, so this is
    envelope tracking, not the round-4 shortcut.
  - But the lead over an equally aligned TRF (+1.0 point) is still within noise.
- Single seed. The SGAT r3 -> EA comparison is between two runs with the same
  seed, so part of it is run-to-run noise (within-subject seed SD ~0.014).
  The seed ensemble in the next round will firm it up.
- Interpretability: after EA, each input "channel" is a symmetric (ZCA)
  whitening mix of electrodes, not one electrode. Channel/edge importance must

Findings
1. BUG, all rounds: StimulusGAT's default electrode graph is built from
   config/dtu_channel_montage.csv, a generic template (Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, ...,
   M1, M2). The DTU EEG columns are in BioSemi-64 order (Fp1, AF7, AF3, F1, ...;
   = config/aadnet_dtu_channel_montage.csv). Only 7/64 names agree by index;
   57 of the graph's 219 edges (26%) join true 6-nearest neighbours; median edge
   length 0.074 vs 0.029 for true neighbours (2-D montage units, m).
   - Effect: the accuracies are real measurements of the trained models, but the
     graph was not the physical prior described; any spatial explanation from
     these models would be wrong.
   - The adjacency mask is a saved buffer, so old checkpoints still reproduce.
   - The same template is the default in stgcn/adjacency.py and in the earlier
     VLAAI / ST-GCN XAI scripts (scripts/run_focused_xai*.py and others); their
     channel names and regions need checking before reuse. The AADNet XAI path
     was already fixed in f5c8734.

   counterbalanced across subjects. Removing the test and val subjects from
   training tilts each fold's training prior towards the test subjects' talker.

     single-talker folds (the round-4 shortcut model scored 0.82-0.84 in g3_k0,

     training-majority talker 50.8% of the time; fold accuracy is unrelated to

   - Class-balanced accuracy does not remove this effect, because each test
     block is single-class.

- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_story_disjoint_v15.py (kernel
  dulanamanjitha/story-disjoint-v15-round10-emab, code at commit 10ab0d6)
- story_disjoint_v15emaB.json and per-fold npz (Kaggle output; untracked).
  Window meta checked identical to round 9 fixB in all 18 folds.

Method / Protocol
- Round-9 corrected baseline, seeds 3 and 4. Each training run keeps two
  candidates: raw weights and an exponential moving average of the weights
  (decay 1 - 1/steps_per_epoch, about 0.993; equal-weight running mean before
  the horizon). Each candidate picks its own best epoch by class-balanced
  pooled val accuracy. Both come from the same run, so the comparison is paired.
- Pre-registered (notebook header): KEEP iff mean selected val over the 36
  (fold, seed) runs is >= 0.5 points higher for EMA. If KEEP, emaA (seeds 42,
  1, 2) completes the 5 seeds. Test results descriptive only.

Results
- Screen (validation): raw 0.7068, EMA 0.7072, diff +0.0004 (EMA higher in
  17/36 runs, lower in 14/36).
- Mean val curve (epochs 1,2,3,4,5,7,10,15,20,25):
    raw 0.681 0.688 0.690 0.687 0.685 0.683 0.676 0.664 0.664 0.660
    EMA 0.680 0.693 0.693 0.696 0.695 0.689 0.684 0.673 0.669 0.668
  EMA slows the decline after the peak but does not raise the peak the
  selection rule picks. Median best epoch raw 4, EMA 5.
- Test (descriptive; 2-seed ensemble, EMA - raw): 5 s +0.0000, 10 s +0.0002,
  20 s +0.0051, 40 s +0.0028, 1 s +0.0038, 2 s +0.0044; no p < 0.05. Single
  seeds at 5 s: +0.0043 (s3), +0.0071 (s4). The one uncorrected p < 0.05
  (s4, 1 s, p = 0.031) is one of 18 descriptive tests and does not survive Holm.
- Reproducibility: the raw arm matches round 9 fixB exactly (window decision
  agreement 1.000 for both seeds; 5 s accuracy 0.6885 / 0.6891).
- Controls (EMA ensemble): shift 0.504 / 0.508 / 0.518 / 0.518, swap 0.498 /
  0.506 / 0.507 / 0.501 at 5/10/20/40 s, all p >= 0.24; max fold at 5 s 0.555
  (shift), 0.561 (swap).

DECISION: REJECT. Round-9 baseline unchanged; emaA not run (saves ~4 GPU-h).

Notes
- Weight averaging does not fix the early, noisy checkpoint selection. The
  noise is probably in the 2-subject validation estimate, not in the weights.
  More val subjects or selection on a pooled criterion would test that, but
  this protocol fixes 2 val subjects per fold.
- Independently of the decision, the raw arm repeats the round-9 code path
  bit for bit on Kaggle.

Entry date: 2026-11-08
Experiment: Interpretability X1 — faithfulness of StimulusGAT explanations (round-9 models)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_xai_round9.py (kernel dulanamanjitha/xai-round9-faithfulness,
  harness at commit c05ebe1), on the round-9 checkpoints (seed 42 explained; all 5
  seeds for stability).
- xai_round9.json (Kaggle output; untracked). 54 subject-folds, 18 subjects, 205 min.
  Saved test logits reproduced exactly (agreement 1.000); median alignment
  locality 0.83.

Method / Protocol
- Per test subject, explanations from one half of the test trials are evaluated
  on the other half (cross-fitted). Subject = unit (n = 18).
- Channel methods: occlusion, integrated gradients (phase-randomised baseline),
  attention received. Edge methods: attention, attention x gradient. Bands: IG on
  the frequency encoder's band-power features, against the margin drop when the
  band is filtered out of all electrodes (band-stop).
- Pre-registered rules in the notebook header (levels 1, 2, 4, bands, H2).

Results
- Level 1, sanity (median Spearman with the fully randomised model, pass if
  |rho| < 0.3): occlusion -0.213, IG -0.072, attention +0.286, band -0.095. All
  pass; attention only just. Randomising the readout leaves attention unchanged
  (+1.00), and randomising everything leaves rho about 0.3, so part of the
  attention pattern does not come from learned weights.
- Level 2, channel faithfulness (deletion by swapping in another window's
  signal; area under the accuracy curve, random = 20 random rankings):
    method     delete  random  Holm p    keep   random  Holm p
    occlusion  0.557   0.607   <0.0001   0.539  0.525   0.004
    IG         0.577   0.607   <0.0001   0.536  0.525   0.013
    attention  0.588   0.607   <0.0001   0.543  0.525   <0.0001
  All PASS. Zero-deletion (secondary) agrees.
- Edges (edge removal): attention 0.674 and attention x gradient 0.669 vs random
  0.693, both Holm p < 0.0001. PASS.
- H2 (attention less faithful): attention deletion area above occlusion
  (p < 0.0001) and above IG (p = 0.019). SUPPORTED.
- Bands: median Spearman between band attribution and band-stop drop -0.13
  (IQR -0.36 to 0.07). FAIL. Band-power IG is small and negative in every band
  (-0.002 to -0.005). Band-stop margin drops: theta 0.167, alpha1 0.117,
  alpha2 0.100, delta 0.053, beta1 0.020, gamma1 0.010, beta3 0.005, beta2 0.004.
- Level 4, stability across the 10 seed pairs (pass if median rho >= 0.7):
  occlusion 0.696, IG 0.567, attention 0.385. All FAIL (occlusion just).
- Group electrode importance (descriptive; z-scored per subject, averaged):
    occlusion  Fp2, Fp1, Fpz, AF7, AF8, AF3, AFz, AF4, T8, PO8
    IG         C5, FC6, T8, FCz, C3, C6, Fz, P8, FC4, TP8
    attention  C5, FCz, C3, POz, F2, C4, FC6, AF3, T8, PO3

DECISION (pre-registered): channel and edge explanations are faithful in the
deletion sense (level 2) and pass sanity (level 1). Attention is the least
faithful channel method (H2). The band-power attribution does not reflect how
the model uses frequency bands. No channel method is stable across seeds (level 4).

Notes
- The most faithful channel method, occlusion, ranks frontal-polar electrodes
  (Fp1/Fp2/Fpz/AF7/AF8) highest. Our pipeline does no EOG removal
  (dtu_dataset.py keeps the first 64 of 73 columns: 64 EEG + 8 ExG + status), so
  the model may use ocular activity. Eye movements are reported to track
  attended speech, so this would be attention-related but not cortical. A
  second possibility is that swapping high-variance frontal channels perturbs
  the model more for reasons unrelated to information. Not tested yet. Needs an
  ocular control (EOG-only decoder; EOG regression and retraining) before any
  neural interpretation of the maps.
- IG and attention agree with each other on fronto-central/temporal sites (C5,
  FCz, C3, T8, FC6), the usual auditory topography, but disagree with occlusion.
- The band IG covers only the frequency-branch path. Band-stop removes the band
  from the temporal branch too, so the two measure different things. The model
  depends on theta and alpha by intervention, and its band-power features
  contribute little. For CLAUDE.md "frequency-band contributions", band-stop is
  the defensible report; the band-power IG should not be presented as one.
- Stability failure: explanations from one seed do not transfer to another.
  Group maps should be averaged over seeds and reported with that spread.

Experiment: StimulusGAT under the CA-TCN / AADNet subject-independent protocol (seed 42, no EA)
Sources:
- notebooks/kaggle_run_dtu_loso_catcn_protocol.py (kernels
  dulanamanjitha/loso-ca-tcn-protocol-a / -b, code at commit c155085); 4.9 h and
  5.1 h GPU.
- loso_catcn_{A,B}.json and 144 per-fold npz (Kaggle output; untracked).

Method / Protocol
- Folds from cv_splits.loso_attended_track_out, tested equal to AADNet's public
  createSICrossValidation + trainLOSO split. For each test subject: KFold(8,
  shuffle, random_state = subject index) over its 60 two-talker trials. Training
  is the other 17 subjects minus trials whose attended wav is attended in the test
  fold (42-71 trials excluded per fold; 759-782 kept). Validation is a random 20%
  of those, from the same training subjects.
- Model: round-9 corrected baseline WITHOUT Euclidean alignment (CA-TCN uses
  none). 5 s training windows with a 2.5 s hop, up to 25 epochs, early stopping
  with patience 5 on class-balanced val accuracy.
- Scoring as AADNet's setWindowSize: decision windows of L s with 50% overlap; 1 s
  and 2 s scored directly by the 5 s-trained model; L >= 5 s by summing 5 s logit
  differences. Accuracy per fold, mean over 8 folds, mean over 18 subjects.
- Pre-registered (notebook header): 95% bootstrap CI over subjects; "above" /
  "below" CA-TCN if its published mean lies below / above our CI, else "level".
  Controls at 5 s: subject Wilcoxon vs 0.5 p >= 0.05 and no subject >= 0.6.

Results (mean over 18 subjects; single model)
  L     ours    95% CI          CA-TCN  AADNet  Ridge   ours-CA-TCN  verdict
  1 s   0.5603  [0.551, 0.570]  0.583   0.575   0.560   -0.023       below
  2 s   0.6094  [0.592, 0.627]  0.634   0.621   0.601   -0.025       below
  5 s   0.6781  [0.650, 0.706]  0.703   0.694   0.656   -0.025       level
  10 s  0.7368  [0.702, 0.772]  0.767   0.758   0.702   -0.030       level
  25 s  0.8247  [0.784, 0.864]  0.858   0.843   0.781   -0.033       level
  50 s  0.8819  [0.838, 0.921]  0.925   0.909   0.836   -0.043       below
  (20 s 0.8050, 40 s 0.8748; no published values.)
- Controls at 5 s: shifted 0.497 (p = 0.23, max subject 0.524), swapped 0.500
  (p = 0.81, max 0.538). Clean.
- Training: best epoch median 6, epochs run median 11 (max 22, never the 25
  cap); val balanced 0.718 (val is from training subjects).
- Per subject at 5 s: S1 0.698, S2 0.682, S3 0.635, S4 0.695, S5 0.706, S6 0.609,
  S7 0.791, S8 0.732, S9 0.638, S10 0.624, S11 0.538, S12 0.645, S13 0.725,
  S14 0.735, S15 0.777, S16 0.630, S17 0.669, S18 0.675.

DECISION (pre-registered): below CA-TCN at 1, 2 and 50 s; level at 5, 10 and
25 s. Not state of the art under CA-TCN's protocol.

Notes
- The point estimate is below CA-TCN at every length (-2.3 to -4.3 points) and
  below AADNet at every length. The "level" verdicts come from wide CIs (subject
  SD 0.06-0.09 at L >= 5 s), not from parity.
- Our story-disjoint parity with CA-TCN (round 9) needed EA and a 5-seed
  ensemble. Without EA and as a single model, the extra training data here (17
  subjects, most stories) does not make up the difference.
- Remaining protocol differences from CA-TCN: we score 1 s / 2 s with a model
  trained on 5 s windows; training hop 2.5 s (CA-TCN 75% overlap); early
  stopping on balanced val accuracy (CA-TCN val loss) with a 25-epoch cap (not
  reached); our preprocessing (1-31 Hz EEG, power-law sub-band envelope).
- No EOG removal in either pipeline as far as AADNet's loader shows; see the
  interpretability X1 entry for the frontal-polar occlusion result.
